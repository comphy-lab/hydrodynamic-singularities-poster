# Content Notes

Working poster frame:

- Hydrodynamic singularities are places where smooth free-surface flows focus
  geometry, stress, and time scales into a tiny region.
- Student-facing examples: drop pinch-off, coalescence, sheet and jet breakup,
  bubble bursting, and viscoelastic beads-on-a-string.
- Keep equations sparse: one scaling relation or dimensionless group per panel.
- Use real Basilisk / CoMPhy figures once the narrative is fixed.

Likely final panel structure:

1. What becomes singular?
2. How do theory and simulations resolve the near-singular region?
3. Why does a tiny neck, bridge, rim, or filament control the whole flow?
