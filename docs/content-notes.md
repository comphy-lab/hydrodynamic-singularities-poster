# Content Notes

Current poster frame:

- A singularity is introduced as a smooth physical process that blows up: a
  finite quantity becomes infinite at a finite time or place.
- The poster should make the memory-loss idea explicit: close to singularity,
  the flow becomes universal and self-similar.
- The main comparison is drop pinch-off versus bubble pinch-off.
- For drops, polymers kill the singularity and produce a beads-on-a-string
  filament.
- For bubbles, polymers leave the singular pinch-off sequence almost unchanged.
- Keep equations sparse: one scaling-law breadcrumb at the end.

Current panel structure:

1. What is Singularity?
2. Drops: elasticity changes the route.
3. Bubbles: elasticity leaves the singularity intact.
4. Why singularities matter, including numerical snapshots and scaling-law
   blurb.

## Visual Direction

- Follow the older Vatsal poster style: large centred title, abstract/highlights
  near the top, one large middle figure strip, concise lower panels, and a clean
  institutional footer.
- Follow the Solidifying Jets poster style for the rounded blue section frames.
- Footer affiliation order: Physics of Fluids left, CoMPhy Lab centred, Durham
  University right.
- Experimental sequence crops deliberately keep only the Newtonian and elastic
  rows; the third row from the source PDFs is ignored.
